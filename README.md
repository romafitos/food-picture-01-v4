## The picture

Tonkotsu ramen shot the way the top of the board likes it — but sharper.
Chopsticks hold a tall, glossy bundle of noodles high above a dark bowl,
broth droplets frozen mid-fall; steam curls through warm side light; a
halved ajitama egg spills its molten golden yolk at the bowl's edge;
seared chashu glistens on dark slate. Two things catch the eye at once —
the lift and the yolk — so the frame reads instantly even at thumbnail
size, the size the pairwise judge compares at.

## Why this, after rounds 1–3

- Round 1 (Tonkotsu, judge 920,359) proved the dish; round 2 (Crispy Pork
  Belly, 864,401) proved a dish change was the wrong lever; round 3
  (dynamic cascade + red chilli-oil ribbon, 910,820) proved that extra
  motion and a red ribbon read as messy, not appetizing.
- This round goes back to round 1's composition — the tall pull the whole
  top 5 shares (943,591, four of them tied) — and beats it on execution
  instead of novelty: a taller, cleaner lift, droplets caught in the air,
  stronger steam, a bigger and more golden yolk, cinematic light. No red
  ribbon, no tangled cascade.
- The alternatives were judged too: a Sukiyaki wagyu-over-yolk close-up
  (the proven #19 line) scored just under the bar, and a chocolate dessert
  and a golden cocktail lost on the unproven-dish axis — the judge's
  revealed taste is ramen, so this round stays ramen.
